# Product contract

Target edition: 16 October 2026. Uptime is a complete website uptime and API monitoring product for a US small-business site or application operator replacing UptimeRobot, Pingdom (SolarWinds), Better Stack Uptime or Site24x7, with the monitoring-as-code and transaction depth buyers seek from Checkly, OpenStatus and Hyperping. Prices/budgets are USD, phone defaults are US numbers, and schedules/reports support US time zones and daylight saving.

The product discovers, operates and maintains trustworthy monitoring of the actual customer outcome: HTTP/API, every named protocol below, job heartbeats, multi-region and private checks, browser transactions, expiring assets, actionable alerts and reporting. Oncall owns full responder coordination and Lamp owns customer-facing status communication; their integrations are part of Uptime's monitoring job. No gap in implementation reduces this destination.

This is a target contract, not a release verdict. Every named clause states the buyer behavior and its acceptance journey. The final table distinguishes done, partial and missing at current code; a cited local test does not establish hosted placement, live migration, provider receipt or accessibility certification. Existing clause IDs and the owner-accepted 13 October archive policy and 16 October three-second readiness budget are preserved. This documentation round changes no homepage, interface or runtime behavior.

## Existing guarantees and supported customer journeys

- **HTTP-001 — Signed HTTP monitoring.** Public hosted HTTP(S) and keyword checks run in the Cloudflare Worker; private checks use the separately scoped Node runner. They retain the exact immutable assertion bundle and bounded transport evidence, and require authenticated claims and signed results; the control plane must reject contradictory completion. See [Integrity and authority](#integrity-and-authority) and [Network and credentials](#network-and-credentials). **Acceptance evidence:** Create an HTTP(S) and keyword monitor through the owner API, run scheduled hosted and signed runner work against a controlled HTTPS service, fail its status/text, recover it and inspect the immutable assertions and transport evidence.

- **EVIDENCE-001 — Immutable scheduled evidence.** Definitions, scheduled slots and every retry attempt retain revision, policy, fixed selected probes, runner identity, timings, status, assertions and error category; retries never erase failures, and observation/state/outbox commit atomically. See [Evidence and state](#evidence-and-state) and [Integrity and authority](#integrity-and-authority). **Acceptance evidence:** Through signed claim/result routes submit failure, retry, late and duplicate attempts, restart, and read history; each attempt and its revision remains identifiable and observation/state/outbox either all commit or none do.

- **STATE-001 — Fixed coverage and incident hysteresis.** Missing or expired passing coverage is Unknown, partial failure stays visible, independent-vantage agreement and scheduled confirmation determine Failing, and complete fresh scheduled passing evidence plus recovery threshold is required for recovery; manual checks and maintenance never rewrite observations or scheduled policy. See [Evidence and state](#evidence-and-state). **Acceptance evidence:** Run two assigned network vantages through partial, missing, stale, failing and recovering slots; inspect Unknown/Degraded/Failing and thresholds. A manual pass, absent probe or maintenance expiry cannot fabricate recovery.

- **AUTHORITY-001 — Server-side scoped authority.** Every private document, read, mutation and export requires server-side operator or scoped runner authority, missing configuration fails closed, browser mutations enforce configured origin, and expired/foreign attempts cannot alter current evidence, and duplicate attempt identities cannot overwrite it; a signature identifies a key holder rather than proving truthful observation. See [Integrity and authority](#integrity-and-authority). **Acceptance evidence:** Open private documents and call native/framework APIs while signed out, revoked, cross-origin or using a foreign/expired runner signature; require refusal with no product writes, then succeed with the authorized session or scoped runner.

- **NETWORK-001 — Pinned bounded destination execution.** Every public DNS answer and redirect hop is validated fail closed; private-runner numeric sockets preserve Host and TLS identity, hosted checks use Cloudflare’s public Internet fetch transport, unsafe/mixed addresses and HTTPS downgrade are rejected, cross-origin redirects strip request authority, and synthetic grants and protected origin-bound local credential files retain their separate custody limits. See [Network and credentials](#network-and-credentials). **Acceptance evidence:** Exercise real controlled HTTP/TLS endpoints with mixed DNS, rebinding, hostname mismatch, redirects, downgrade, forbidden addresses, credentials and execution bounds; inspect requests and prove rejected destinations receive no connection or authority.

- **HEARTBEAT-001 — Revision-scoped receipt evaluation.** Hashed owner-issued tokens and increasing sequences protect server-time receipts, ingress cannot advance health, and scheduled signed completion must match its exact installation-local claim snapshot; stale, future, paused, foreign-revision or imported receipts cannot establish Healthy. See [Network and credentials](#network-and-credentials). **Acceptance evidence:** Issue and rotate a heartbeat token through the owner door, send increasing receipts, miss one, and evaluate scheduled signed snapshots; replay, old tokens, imported or foreign-revision receipts cannot change current health.

- **ARCHIVE-001 — Inactive validated restore.** Bounded versioned archives require integrity and semantic validation before transactional restore into empty product data, preserve historical evidence, erasure receipts and the permanent anonymised action audit, create paused current revisions, interrupt leases and quarantine alerts; installation-local sessions, keys, grants, request credential values, heartbeat authority, contact, webhook and Slack URLs, and signing keys are excluded; destination definitions and retained notification histories are portable, and imported evidence cannot establish current health or replay events. The source erasure journal fences copies made before a later erasure when that journal accompanies the restore. A local CLI receiving checkout must run setup first to establish its owner and settings. The owner accepted this installation-authority and endpoint-credential archive policy on 2026-10-13; it is an intentional replacement, not BASE-DATA-001 equivalence. See [Portability and retention](#portability-and-retention) and [Integrity and authority](#integrity-and-authority). **Acceptance evidence:** Export a populated installation, establish a different receiving owner, import into empty product data, then export/read history. Definitions are paused, leases interrupted, delivery quarantined, secrets/authority absent, history retained and later erasure journals enforced; corrupt/nonempty imports write nothing.

- **IMPORT-001 — Reviewed definition files and inactive imports.** A versioned definition file (2 MiB, 500 monitors, UTF-8 JSON, editor fields only, no request secrets or installation authority) or saved UptimeRobot v3 monitor pages are previewed without writes as create, unchanged, change, refused or unsupported per entry with a plan digest, validated by the editor's own validation; apply refuses a digest that no longer matches and otherwise writes the whole plan in one transaction as new paused monitors or new revisions that keep enabled, maintenance and runner assignments; nothing in a file enables a monitor, issues a heartbeat token, schedules a notification or touches a destination. Each apply is a batch; undo pauses what it created and restores what it changed while their revisions are unchanged, reports the rest and never removes history. Batches travel in archives as restored history without authority. A log export is counted, never stored or turned into evidence. Unmeasured: no real UptimeRobot export and no hosted run. See [Definition files and imports](#definition-files-and-imports). **Acceptance evidence:** Preview, apply, export and undo definition JSON and saved UptimeRobot pages through the owner routes and CLI. Malformed/hostile/secret-bearing inputs and stale digests write nothing; creations stay paused, changes keep operational state, unsupported fields are named and history is never made current evidence.

- **RETENTION-001 — Explicit storage custody.** Product and observation metadata and legacy development sign-in records have no automatic age/count deletion; operator erasure removes captures for the associated operator, and local reset clears the installation. Expired sessions, authentication/public rate-limit state and short-lived login-attempt counters are cleaned up. Expired or confirmed alert-drill links lose their capability material while drill history stays; retired destination secrets are scrubbed only after owner rotation/deletion and in-flight sends finish; a proven-unsent integration-attempt reservation can be removed while its delivery history remains. Other expired operational records, including discovery jobs, change status and remain stored unless an owner erases the associated entity or resets the installation. Raw target response bodies and probe-request secrets are not retained in observations, webhook and Slack endpoint URLs and signing keys remain installation-local, available email addresses travel as destination definitions, view/archive bounds are not deletion or capacity claims, and retention changes must never silently improve the fixed denominator or historical health. See [Portability and retention](#portability-and-retention). **Acceptance evidence:** Populate product, authentication, rate-limit, drill, credential and attempt records; run expiry, maintenance and restart through runtime doors and compare surviving identities. Only declared transient data/secret material disappears; no cleanup improves uptime or removes retained product history.

- **ERASURE-001 — Operator-directed entity erasure.** An owner can erase a monitor with its evidence and dependent notification/heartbeat records, a destination with its credential copies, or an operator while another active owner remains. Monitor erasure requires typing the current monitor name. Each completed erasure returns a receipt with entity kind, per-table counts and completion time, and returns it only after the transaction containing the erasure, audit entry and portability fence succeeds. Requests based on stale monitor or destination state cannot re-create erased rows. The last active owner cannot be erased: local installations use `rer reset --yes`, while hosted installations require replacement by redeployment. Uptime has no R2 upload feature, so product erasure has no uploaded attachments to remove. Already downloaded archives, messages already sent to external recipients and requests already in progress remain outside local erasure. See [Erasure and audit](#erasure-and-audit). **Acceptance evidence:** Erase each supported entity through the owner API, including stale concurrent requests and the original owner with a successor. Inspect the atomic per-table receipt, audit and journal; the last owner is protected and an old archive with the later journal cannot resurrect erased records.

- **AUDIT-001 — Durable anonymised audit.** Destructive actions, automated notification attempts and operator authentication-mail sends record actor or feature, time, action, subject kind and an opaque subject reference. Audit history is portable and survives erasure of the subject; erasing an operator anonymises its prior actor references. The audit contains no monitor names, target URLs, email addresses or destination URLs. See [Erasure and audit](#erasure-and-audit) and [Portability and retention](#portability-and-retention). **Acceptance evidence:** Perform destructive actions, notification attempts and authentication-mail attempts through public doors, erase an actor and round-trip an archive; inspect durable opaque subjects and anonymized actors without planted names, addresses, URLs or secrets.

- **PRESENCE-001 — Contact separate from health.** Only accepted scoped claims and signed results refresh server-time contact, the 120-second freshness threshold never proves process liveness or target health, and restart, unavailable reads and archive omission preserve that distinction. See [Contact, interface and delivery](#contact-interface-and-delivery). **Acceptance evidence:** Read Never seen, make accepted idle claims/results, age contact, reject foreign requests and restart. Reads and health checks do not refresh contact; unavailable reads and restore cannot imply runner or target health.

- **INTERFACE-001 — Honest durable operator feedback.** Textual state/freshness/reasons, immutable history, labels, keyboard controls and responsive layout expose persisted evidence; failed reads show Unknown, action errors survive polling, busy reconciliation excludes controls and editing rejects assertion overrides. See [Contact, interface and delivery](#contact-interface-and-delivery). **Acceptance evidence:** Use the real owner console at 1440 and 390 with healthy, stale, failing and unavailable monitors; edit assertions, force read/action errors and polling, navigate by keyboard and inspect focus, text state, overflow, accessibility tree and axe results.

- **NOTIFY-001 — Authorized installation-wide destinations.** An owner may configure email, signed JSON webhook, or Slack incoming webhook destinations for all monitors in one installation; only scheduled incident transitions generate automatic notifications, and a test send cannot change monitor evidence or incident state. The hosted Worker submits email through the configured Base mail adapter and sends webhooks and Slack without a private runner; a private runner can also claim notification work. Destination credentials are installation-local and are not restored from archives. See [Contact, interface and delivery](#contact-interface-and-delivery) and [Network and credentials](#network-and-credentials). **Acceptance evidence:** Create/rotate/disable email, Slack and signed webhook destinations, cause scheduled failure/recovery and a test send through actual Worker/runner delivery doors; inspect one job per enabled destination, credentials, exact signing, escaping and unchanged health for tests.

- **DELIVERY-001 — Bounded notification acknowledgement.** Each event and destination gets one automatic send attempt. A RER relay 429 with Retry-After confirms non-acceptance and queues another submission after that delay. HTTP 500, connection loss, timeout, and unknown outcomes remain uncertain and are not automatically sent again. Accepted means the configured provider or endpoint acknowledged the request; it does not mean a person received, read, or acted on it. See [Contact, interface and delivery](#contact-interface-and-delivery). **Acceptance evidence:** At authorized provider edges return accepted, proven-unaccepted relay 429, HTTP 500, timeout and connection loss; restart during claims. Only the proven-unaccepted case requeues automatically; uncertain started sends never replay and acceptance remains distinct from receipt.

- **PUBLIC-001 — Public landing and documentation.** At the app origin, `/` is the unauthenticated Lobby, `/docs` is public documentation, `/setup` is first-run setup, and the private operator console and editor are `/app` and `/app/monitor`; private documents require an owner session. The separate marketing site serves `https://run-uptime.com/` and `https://run-uptime.com/run`. The app Lobby at `/` and the marketing landing at the run-site `/` are distinct surfaces. **Acceptance evidence:** Open app-origin Lobby, docs and setup signed out, then sign in for /app and /app/monitor; separately read the marketing site and /run. Their routes remain distinct and private documents reject unsigned access.

- **BOUNDARY-001 — Monitoring ownership and honest delivery claims.** Uptime owns checks, evidence, routing, repeat reminders and monitoring reports. It feeds full paging and responder coordination to [Oncall](https://github.com/just-every/run-oncall), PagerDuty or Splunk On-Call, and public incidents, branded pages and subscriber updates to [Lamp](https://github.com/just-every/run-lamp) or Atlassian Statuspage. Teams, Discord, SMS, voice and push are supported target channels under the clauses below. One network label is one vantage; provider acknowledgement never proves human receipt or response. Geographic placement, browser execution and accessibility require their own measured evidence. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure an alert and a sibling handoff, inspect acknowledged/received/responded as separate facts and simulate lost probes/providers; no duplicate network labels, acknowledgement or missing evidence becomes a delivery or availability guarantee.

- **DRILL-001 — Recipient-reported alert drills.** An owner can run an alert drill on one destination: the existing test event with drill wording, sent once through the same path with no automatic resend, plus a one-time confirmation link (random capability, stored only as a bound hash, single use, 24-hour expiry, one drill and one destination). Opening the link shows a page; only a same-origin POST confirms, and unknown, used, expired and foreign links answer 404 alike. The console keeps Submitted, Acknowledged (or Uncertain/Failed with its code) and Recipient confirmed as separate lines, and does not measure whether anyone acted. A confirmed drill is a report by the recipient that one message reached a person at one time. It is never evidence of monitoring, a delivery guarantee or a human response, and it changes no monitor evidence, state or incident. Proof belongs to the destination's current credential: a new address or URL or a rotated secret makes it Never proven, and drill history stays with the installation, so a restored destination is Never proven. See [Alert drills](#alert-drills). **Acceptance evidence:** Drill email, Slack and signed webhook through the actual delivery runtime: GET/HEAD never confirm, same-origin POST confirms once, expired/replayed/foreign links refuse, 500 stays uncertain, rotation resets proof and archives/logs contain no capability or endpoint secret.

- **STARTUP-001 — Required configuration diagnostics.** A shared configuration table validates required and conditional bindings for the Worker, scheduled entry point, local app, preview, setup, deployment and private runner before each begins work. Missing or malformed required values stop the entry point and name the variable without printing its value; a Worker configuration failure returns 503, including at `/health`. Hosted mode requires the selected Base mail provider and its sender configuration. See [Configuration diagnostics](#configuration-diagnostics). **Acceptance evidence:** Start each runtime entry point with required and conditional bindings missing/malformed; diagnostics name bindings without values and health returns 503. Restore settings and recover; exercise D1/ASSETS faults inside the owner-approved three-second readiness response budget.

- **PUBLIC-002 — Declared sessionless paths and rate limits.** Every sessionless route and emitted static path is declared in `seed.json` with its authentication rule and rate-limit class. D1 applies owner-tunable per-client limits in fixed 60-second windows and returns 429 with `Retry-After` after the class limit. A declaration does not bypass route-specific session, runner, heartbeat or development-key checks. Limit values are marked estimated and not load-tested. See [Public paths and rate limits](#public-paths-and-rate-limits) and [Integrity and authority](#integrity-and-authority). **Acceptance evidence:** Enumerate declared sessionless paths against emitted routes, exceed configured public limits, inspect Retry-After, and remove counter storage; each route retains its own authentication checks and admission fails closed.

- **FLOW-001 — Supported isolated browser transactions.** Login, signup and checkout are supported monitored customer paths with explicit intended outcomes, immutable revisions, safe synthetic accounts and scheduled consensus. A constrained editor and isolated Playwright execution share the same evidence and authority rules. Every required outcome must pass; missing browser/credentials is Unknown. Per-run process/profile isolation, sanitized environment, network filtering, hard time/byte limits and credential-origin binding apply to all scripts. Human approval is required for effects beyond an explicitly authorized synthetic transaction. A changed-definition recovery is labelled and never presented as proof of the original outcome. The current constrained-flow implementation is partial; support qualification and the additional FLOW clauses remain required. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure supported login, signup and sandbox checkout journeys, run independent real-browser vantages, break required outcomes and recover through signed results. Wrong credentials, missing elements, server errors, slow pages, private redirects/subresources, extra origins and absent browsers have distinct retained outcomes; weakened definitions cannot silently clear the original incident.

## Monitoring and operations

- **MONITOR-001 — Owned monitor catalog and lifecycle.** An operator organizes monitors by project, environment, service, tags, owner and dependencies; bulk create/edit/pause/resume/clone/search/filter preserve revision and incident history. Every monitor names its intended customer outcome, schedule, locations, assertions, maintenance and alert policy. Pausing never erases downtime. **Acceptance evidence:** Create production/staging monitors in the console/API, bulk change a selected tag, clone one, pause/resume another and compare revision, ownership and incident history before/after.

- **API-001 — Complete HTTP and API assertions.** HTTP(S) methods, safe redirects, custom headers/body, expected status sets/ranges, keyword presence and absence, response headers, latency, nested JSONPath and regular-expression assertions evaluate the exact saved bundle with bounded redacted diagnostics. A transport 200 cannot pass a failed business assertion. **Acceptance evidence:** Save checks through the editor/API against a controlled endpoint; independently break absent/present keywords, status range, response headers, JSON arrays/nested paths, regex and latency, then inspect the distinct failures and unchanged saved criteria.

- **API-002 — Authenticated monitoring and secret lifecycle.** Basic/bearer, OAuth 2.0 acquisition/refresh, JWT signing and mTLS client certificates use owner-authorized secret references, least scope, expiry/rotation and exact origins. Test accounts and credentials remain under installation/runner custody; auth errors remain distinguishable from application failures and secrets never enter evidence or exports. **Acceptance evidence:** Use each authentication mode against an authorized endpoint, expire/rotate/revoke it and redirect cross-origin; show expected failure/recovery, refusal of foreign references and no planted secret in storage, logs, browser artifacts or archives.

- **API-003 — Multi-step API transactions.** A scheduled backend transaction shares scoped authentication, variables and extracted response values across ordered requests, asserts each business outcome and records per-step timing. Setup, failure and cleanup remain visible; a later success cannot erase an earlier failed requirement. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a login/create/read/delete API journey through the supported configuration door, fail a middle assertion and expire shared auth, then inspect step history and cleanup without leaking extracted secrets.

- **API-004 — Safe setup and teardown.** Browser and API journeys run bounded setup/teardown fixtures using synthetic accounts, Stripe test mode and buyer sandbox resources; cleanup runs after failure or cancellation and leaves durable actionable cleanup debt when it cannot complete. Live purchases, destructive writes or refunds require an exact time-limited grant and spend limit. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a sandbox checkout with setup/teardown, cancel mid-flow and deny cleanup at the network edge; inspect bounded attempts, retained cleanup failure and explicit operator resolution without a live charge.

- **TCP-001 — TCP service checks.** TCP monitors test a chosen host/port, handshake latency and optional bounded banner from selected locations with the same confirmation, evidence and alert policy as HTTP. Refused connections, timeout and probe failure remain distinct. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a TCP monitor through the console/API; open, close and blackhole an authorized port from two locations, then read evidence and trigger/recovery events.

- **PING-001 — ICMP reachability and packet quality.** ICMP ping monitors retain bounded packet loss, round-trip latency and configured thresholds by location for IPv4/IPv6. ICMP blocked by policy is shown explicitly rather than inferred from HTTP success. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure a ping target, vary loss and latency, disable ICMP and stop a probe; inspect protocol-specific results and Unknown probe coverage.

- **DNS-001 — DNS records and change detection.** Checks query selected recursive and authoritative resolvers for A, AAAA, CNAME, MX, TXT, NS, SOA, SRV and CAA records, expected values, absence/change and response time. NXDOMAIN, SERVFAIL, propagation differences and monitor infrastructure faults are distinguishable; owner-approved expected changes suppress only their own condition. **Acceptance evidence:** Save DNS monitors via the owner door, change/remove an expected record and induce resolver disagreement/failure; inspect raw bounded record evidence, confirmed alerts, maintenance and recovery without treating a missing answer as success.

- **TLS-001 — SSL certificate and chain monitoring.** TLS checks verify hostname/SNI, certificate chain/trust, validity, expiry and renewal on HTTPS or configured TLS ports from each location. Staged configurable expiry warnings name the certificate and accountable owner, track renewal and resolve only after a newly observed valid chain. **Acceptance evidence:** Monitor a controlled TLS service; present an expiring certificate, broken chain, wrong name and then renewed certificate, advance the clock and inspect deduplicated warnings, location evidence and renewal resolution.

- **DOMAIN-001 — Domain registration expiry.** Domain monitors use RDAP and authorized registrar/WHOIS data, retain source/freshness, warn the owner before registration expiry and follow through until renewal is independently observed. Redacted, unavailable or conflicting registry data is Unknown; certificate renewal is not domain renewal. **Acceptance evidence:** Add a domain in the console, drive authorized registry-edge responses through approaching expiry, unavailable data, conflicting dates and renewal; inspect distinct warnings, source times and resolved follow-up.

- **UDP-001 — UDP request and response checks.** Bounded UDP checks validate configured payload/response and timing on an authorized host/port with anti-abuse traffic limits and location consensus. No response is not treated as proof of an open port. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a UDP monitor, return matching/mismatching payloads, drop packets and remove probe authority; inspect timeout versus assertion versus runner failures and bounded outbound traffic.

- **MAIL-001 — SMTP and mailbox protocol checks.** SMTP/STARTTLS, POP3/POP3S and IMAP/IMAPS checks verify connection, TLS, banner, authentication and optional synthetic send/receive transaction with a dedicated test mailbox. Message IDs, timing and cleanup are retained without mailbox content or passwords; a successful SMTP accept is distinct from mailbox receipt. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure each protocol via the owner door against authorized mail edges, break authentication/TLS and delay receipt; inspect per-step alerts, receiver confirmation and removal of synthetic messages.

- **SCHEDULE-001 — Explicit cadence, retries and confirmation.** Each revision records interval, timeout, retry budget, freshness, failure/recovery slot thresholds, fixed selected probes and assignments. Scheduled work cannot count retries as independent slots, invent missed checks or let manual runs alter cadence. Outages shorter than the sampling interval may be missed. **Acceptance evidence:** Save a cadence through the API/CLI, run retries, delay claims, edit the interval and make a manual pass; inspect due times, fixed coverage and incident thresholds through history.

- **PROBE-001 — US and global independent locations.** Buyers select managed US East, Central and West plus Europe and Asia-Pacific locations, see operator/network/region identity and IPv4/IPv6 coverage, and configure duration/location consensus. A lone network blip never fires a confirmed outage; independent target observations or an independently passing canary corroborate it. Probe health and regional partial failure remain visible; no label pretends to be physical independence. **Acceptance evidence:** Run an authorized public target from real US and global locations, break only one probe network and then the target, stop a region and recover. Inspect independent network evidence, Degraded/Unknown coverage and confirmed trigger/recovery.

- **PRIVATE-001 — Buyer-network private locations.** Scoped buyer-owned runners monitor allowlisted intranet/VPC services with outbound-only control-plane access, revocable identity, capacity/upgrade/health visibility and secret locality. Explicit per-runner private hostname/CIDR/port grants are separate from public probes; metadata and management endpoints remain blocked. Datadog private-location users can deploy equivalent Uptime runners beside existing agents without forwarding Datadog credentials or misrepresenting its probes as ours. **Acceptance evidence:** Enroll a runner in an authorized private network, grant one service, execute HTTP/TCP/browser checks, then revoke it. Unauthorized neighbors, metadata, redirected private addresses and public-runner use of the grant refuse without connecting.

- **MAINT-001 — Planned and recurring maintenance.** One-time and recurring windows target monitors/groups with US time zones, daylight-saving handling, preview and audit. Buyers choose notification suppression or execution pause explicitly; reports show maintenance separately and retain denominator and underlying observations. Expiry re-evaluates actual evidence and cannot emit orphan recovery. **Acceptance evidence:** Schedule a New York recurring maintenance window across a DST transition and a one-time group window; fail a monitor during each, restart, end/cancel the window and inspect preserved results, suppression and reports.

- **REPORT-001 — Latency, uptime and SLA reporting.** Per-monitor/service/location charts show response-time percentiles, availability, outage duration, incident timelines and SLA/SLO attainment for buyer-chosen periods and US time zones. Export CSV/JSON/PDF and schedule email/Slack reports to authorized recipients. Unknown, partial, paused and maintenance time and denominator policy are visible; imported history remains sourced and cannot improve current health. **Acceptance evidence:** Cause known failures, gaps, slow responses, maintenance and revisions; compare custom-period charts and downloaded reports to immutable slots, schedule a report, fail delivery and inspect its durable outcome.

- **DIAG-001 — Connection and network diagnostics.** A confirmed timeout can collect bounded traceroute/MTR, DNS, TCP and TLS phase timings from authorized probe locations, retain hop loss/latency and compare healthy regions. Redaction and traffic caps apply; blocked diagnostics are explicitly unavailable and never alter the monitor verdict. **Acceptance evidence:** Trigger a controlled route/connect timeout, open diagnostics from incident detail and compare two locations with one ICMP-blocked path; inspect bounded artifacts, visible unavailable reason and unchanged incident.

- **PERF-001 — Page speed and real-user performance.** Synthetic page-speed checks retain DNS/connect/TLS/TTFB, resource waterfall and Core Web Vitals budgets. An optional consent-aware browser beacon measures real-user availability/performance by route, device and region with sampling, privacy exclusions and bot filtering; RUM and synthetic denominators remain distinct. Buyers can find a slow mobile checkout despite a healthy health endpoint. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure a page-speed monitor and consented beacon on a controlled site, throttle mobile assets and produce a browser error; inspect waterfall, budget alert, privacy/sampling settings and separate RUM versus synthetic reports.

- **DEPENDENCY-001 — External dependency checks.** Read-only discovery proposes third-party status components or public status endpoints as separate monitors with source attribution and reviewed assertions. A provider status claim is contextual evidence rather than proof that the buyer application is available. **Acceptance evidence:** Discover a linked provider status endpoint through the owner journey, review/adopt its proposed monitor and change the provider component; inspect its independent evidence without changing the application monitor.

- **FLOW-002 — Reusable Playwright monitoring as code.** Buyers import and version real Playwright scripts, test suites and fixtures for login, signup and checkout alongside API checks, with dependency locks, selected secrets, setup/teardown and safe effects grants. Each run is isolated outside ordinary Worker requests; scripts cannot bypass destination or resource controls. Editor and code revisions expose the same required outcomes. **Acceptance evidence:** Import a real Playwright suite via CLI/GitHub Actions, preview and deploy it, then break a fixture, assertion and forbidden network call; scheduled runs preserve intended outcomes, isolation and reviewable revision history.

- **FLOW-003 — Useful and safe browser failure artifacts.** Failed steps retain redacted screenshots, Playwright traces, video replay, console errors, network/timing waterfalls and step durations tied to the immutable run. Owner-defined masks, no secret-bearing headers/bodies and bounded retention/export/access prevent synthetic credentials or personal data leaking into artifacts. Artifact-generation failure is visible and cannot hide the check failure. **Acceptance evidence:** Break sandbox checkout, inspect screenshot/video/trace/waterfall through authenticated incident detail, deny artifact storage and try an unauthorized download. Planted secrets/masked fields never appear; retained artifact failures and erasure are visible.

- **FLOW-004 — Cross-browser and mobile transactions.** A buyer selects Chromium, Firefox or WebKit and desktop/mobile viewport/device profiles per journey; reports and consensus preserve that browser/device dimension. Missing browser capacity stays Unknown. Native iOS/Android app testing is a separate device-testing integration; mobile web paths are Uptime checks. **Acceptance evidence:** Deploy the same checkout to all three browsers and a 390-pixel mobile profile, introduce a mobile-only failure and remove one browser; inspect per-profile failures and Unknown capacity without averaging them into success.

- **ROUTING-001 — Monitor, service and team alert routing.** Route confirmed alerts by monitor/group, environment, severity, region, incident origin, team and US business/quiet hours; select multiple contacts with explicit inheritance, preview and test. Destination secrets stay installation-local and routing changes are auditable. A paused or disabled contact cannot silently count as covered. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure two teams and production/staging routes through the UI/API; trigger each class inside/outside quiet hours, rotate a destination and inspect selected/excluded recipients and drill outcomes.

- **FOLLOWUP-001 — Ongoing outage follow-up and correlation.** Each confirmed incident has an owner, next-due follow-up, durable progress and stop/resolution state. Configurable repeated reminders are new auditable events with stable incident identity; they never replay an uncertain send under DELIVERY-001. Correlate simultaneous dependency failures for grouped acknowledgement while preserving every monitor result; acknowledgement stops reminders only under the selected policy, and recovery closes the right incident. **Acceptance evidence:** Trigger correlated outages, acknowledge one group, restart before a due reminder, lose a delivery response and recover one child; inspect one event per due reminder, retained uncertainty, unclosed children and final resolution without duplicate sends.

- **CHAT-001 — Teams, Discord and workplace chat.** Native Microsoft Teams, Discord, Google Chat and Mattermost destinations carry monitor, location, failed assertion, severity, incident key and authenticated evidence link with tested trigger/recovery/drill formatting. Per-channel scopes, credential rotation, rate limits and delivery uncertainty follow the same custody rules as Slack. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure each named connector through the owner console, drill it, fail and recover a monitor, return a provider error and rotate credentials; inspect channel readback, correlation and distinct acknowledgement/receipt states.

- **PHONE-001 — US SMS and voice alerts.** Twilio-backed SMS and voice calls to verified US E.164 numbers deliver incident context and secure acknowledgement with consent, quiet hours, opt-out, per-team USD budgets and visible provider/carrier outcomes. Repeats and call escalation follow the buyer policy and Oncall handoff; Uptime does not act as a carrier or guarantee receipt. **Acceptance evidence:** Verify a US test number in the authorized provider sandbox, trigger SMS/voice and acknowledgement, simulate opt-out, spend cap, failed carrier and uncertain call result; inspect no unauthorized sends/charges and visible unresolved follow-up.

- **MOBILE-001 — Mobile operator access and push.** Responsive operator flows and native iOS/Android clients support secure sign-in, monitor/incident evidence, pause/maintenance controls and push alerts via APNs/FCM; Pushover and Pushbullet are selectable provider destinations. ## Declared external data flows and baseline replacements

`seed.json → externals` declares the source-level outbound data categories. Public HTTP(S) monitors and status-only canaries resolve each public target, validate every redirect hop, and send the configured method, path/query, allowed headers and request body to the target. A request credential is sent only to its exact owner-authorized monitor origin; cross-origin redirects strip it, custom headers and body. Ordinary checks read bounded response status, headers and body for assertions without retaining raw bodies in observations. A canary is a status-only `GET` with no credential, custom headers or body and reads zero response-body bytes. DNS queries reveal only the hostnames being resolved plus source network address and lookup timing to the resolver configured for the runner.

Discovery is read-only and runs on the owner's runner. It may follow any validated public redirect from the initial URL; subsequent crawls are bounded to the final/input registrable domains and linked status hosts, subject to robots and request/time/body limits. It may request robots files, permitted pages, JSON/spec documents, conventional API hosts and status hosts. Bounded response content is parsed in runner memory. The Worker stores only the bounded derived result. No supplied HTML is rendered as console HTML.

## C3 — Stored evidence and independent failure agreement

A slot outcome and scheduled confirmation/recovery streaks are pure projections of stored attempts, recalculated on each arrival in chronological slot order. A derived checkpoint folds only expired acceptance windows and keeps exact, unclamped counters; complete slots still accepting late attempts remain mutable. Reads re-evaluate current freshness without advancing the checkpoint counters. Failing requires terminal target failures from two distinct runner network labels, or one failing vantage with its passing canary in the same slot. A failed canary counts its network absent; its failure remains Degraded. Missing/expired evidence is Unknown; recovery requires complete fresh passing coverage. Same-label runners count once. New enabled production monitor settings refuse configurations incapable of reaching Failing: a public monitor on one network label with no external check URL of its own takes the installation's External check URL (an owner-only setting on Runner / archive, seeded with `https://example.org/`) into its saved revision, so it is refused only when the setting is absent. An external check on the monitored site itself (the same hostname, ignoring case and a leading `www.`) is refused whether it is the default or the owner's own, because it would fail whenever the monitor does and a failure could never be confirmed. Revisions are immutable, so changing the setting applies to monitors saved afterwards and never rewrites a saved one. The setting is installation-local and does not travel in archives; the URL a revision carries does. Assignment-free historical revisions keep their saved terminal-probe failure quorum until the owner saves a new revision; the overview and count-only owner notices flag the need for a second independent network or an external check URL. `tests/resilience/attempts.mjs` proves these flows through signed Worker routes and real probes at local network edges.

## C4 — Per-runner assigned attempts

Each runner has an independently revocable key and a declared network label. A revision assigns each probe to a runner. The immutable attempt identity is slot/probe/attempt number; only that assigned, unrevoked runner can submit it inside the acceptance window. The first accepted copy wins. Results have no slot-wide lease owner and may contain any assigned subset. Stored evidence, recalculated state and incident events commit atomically under a generation guard. Migration preserves the existing runner as the first row. C5's pinned resolution remains unchanged. `tests/resilience/attempts.mjs` covers migration, signatures, assignments, duplicates, expiry, order and revocation through public routes.


## Evidence and state

Every scheduled slot and attempt retains its monitor revision, policy, selected probes, runner version, start/completion, status, assertion results and error category. Retries are attempts inside a slot and never erase an earlier failure or increment scheduled confirmation/recovery streaks. Raw target response bodies and probe-request secrets are not retained in observations; notification destination credentials remain installation-local and are omitted from portable archives.

The 24-hour and 7-day history coverage includes complete scheduled results across revisions, evaluated with each immutable revision’s settings and bounded by its next revision. Maintenance and paused periods are shown separately from observed and Unknown time; historical results cannot establish current-revision health. Last check retains the latest completed scheduled observation across revisions.

Target evidence, coverage, and notification delivery are independent. Healthy requires fresh, complete selected-probe coverage and the configured recovery threshold. Incomplete passing coverage is Unknown. A fresh failure without independent-vantage agreement is Degraded; meeting the C3 agreement and confirmation threshold is Failing even when coverage is incomplete. Expired evidence becomes Unknown without silently resolving an open incident. Recovery requires complete fresh passing scheduled slots. Runner errors and absent observations remain distinct from target failure. Scheduled incident transitions and their destination jobs are committed with the corresponding observation and state change; a test send is separate from that history.

Maintenance suppresses event emission while preserving observations and target state. After maintenance expires, a real completed slot may open a still-confirmed failure. Recovery requires a previously open trigger, preventing orphan recoveries. Pending means stored locally. Manual checks do not change scheduled cadence, confirmation or recovery.

## Integrity and authority

Each Node runner signs purpose-separated enrolment and results with its own scoped key. Identity, declared network label, revocation and credentials stay installation-local. Legacy key-only clients resolve to the migrated first runner; they have no authority over other runners' probes. See C4 above.

Slot identity is monitor/revision/due time; attempt identity includes slot/probe/attempt. Manual requests have distinct identities. The acceptance window is bounded; expired, foreign or contradictory attempts are rejected and the first accepted copy wins. Edited or paused inflight work remains historical evidence and cannot change current state or events. Missed cadence is counted without fabricated catchup results. A scheduled check is due no sooner than one interval after the monitor's previous scheduled check was claimed and after its first attempt reported starting; after an edit, the shorter of the old and new intervals applies, so a late check moves later due times back rather than running checks closer together; equal runner and Worker clocks make the reported-start floor exact. Backward clock movement waits for due time; future evidence cannot establish health.

Observation, state and outbox writes share one durable transaction. Migration history is permanent. Completion verifies the immutable definition, fixed probe denominator, exact assertion bundle, outcome, clock bounds and transport limits. Live latency validation uses the larger retained elapsed measure. Historical archive compatibility does not relax live validation.

Every private document, mutation, observation read and export requires server-side operator or scoped runner authority. Missing configuration fails closed. Browser mutations bind the configured origin and reject cross-site requests. Loopback is not authentication. Shared Base operator-auth owns operator credentials and sessions; product policy must not silently grant additional roles. Signed reporting identifies the holder of the runner key; it does not prove an uncompromised runner.

## Network and credentials

Private-runner public HTTP(S) checks validate every A/AAAA answer and redirect hop, reject unsafe or mixed address sets, pin numeric sockets and retain original Host, SNI and certificate hostname verification. Proxy environments are bypassed. HTTPS downgrade is rejected; an origin change strips credentials, custom headers, request body and mutating method. DNS, redirects, body and retries have bounded execution. Responses are limited to 64 KiB; compressed responses are rejected. An external check asserts status 200 only, so it stops at the response headers and neither reads nor keeps the body, however large the page; every other guarantee here applies to it unchanged. Independent network isolation remains an operating requirement. Hosted HTTP(S) and keyword monitors without runner-local credentials default to the Cloudflare Worker. Owner-console requests prompt due work; its one-minute Cron schedule continues background work, but initial Cloudflare activation can take up to about two hours. The Worker validates all public DNS answers and every redirect and uses Cloudflare’s public Internet fetch transport with certificate verification, no private-network binding, no cached probe response and the same time, body and assertion bounds. Numeric socket pinning is the private runner’s transport guarantee. The Worker is one network vantage; it uses the same configured canary and scheduled evidence rules and cannot supply independent regional agreement. Other checks require an assigned private runner.

Synthetic checks retain a separate live process/custody grant, exact path/port identity, expiry, GET/HEAD-only and no-redirect boundary. Grant authority is rechecked on every attempt.

Request headers, body and bearer/basic credentials are protected runner-local regular files with exact monitor-ID and origin binding. Credential authority comes from the authenticated task envelope while its assigned probe and acceptance window are valid; another monitor on the same runner cannot use the same reference to obtain the credential. Setup runs numbered local-file migration 0001: valid unbound files used by exactly one existing monitor bind automatically, counting paused monitors; shared and unused files remain unbound with explicit owner instructions. Scheduled checks record bounded non-secret binding diagnostics and never bind files. Missing or invalid references yield runner errors without falling back to unauthenticated requests. Raw secrets and response bodies never enter observations, alerts or archives. Portable historical references are not receiving-installation execution authority.

Heartbeat tokens are owner-issued, hashed at rest, scoped to an immutable revision and rotated on issuance. Server receipt time and strictly increasing sequences prevent replay. Ingress alone cannot change state. A scheduled slot freezes an installation-local receipt snapshot; signed completion must match it. Missing, stale, future, paused, revision-mismatched or imported receipts cannot establish Healthy.

## Flows

The current constrained browser-flow code is not yet qualified as supported. FLOW-001 through FLOW-004 define the supported target; the following describes the existing constrained implementation and its retained safety obligations.

A flow is validated by the same function in the Worker, the definitions engine and the runner. Steps are `goto`, `click`, `fill` (a `credentialRef`, never a value), `waitFor` and `expect`; a target is exactly one of a plain CSS selector, text, label, or a role with its name. Required outcomes have an id and an expectation, and every one must be observed after the last step. Unknown step kinds and fields, scripts, other selector engines, secrets, intervals under 5 minutes, more than 20 steps or 8 outcomes, and more than 5 flows in an installation are refused with the entry named. Bounds per run: 60 seconds of wall time, 8 top-level pages, 5 MiB of response bytes, an 8 KiB trace; exceeding one fails the run and names it.

The runner serves a flow only through `runners/src/flow/`: its own process, a fresh 0700 profile deleted afterwards, an environment holding no inherited credential, proxy setting or Node hook, and the named credentials passed over a pipe. Chromium's only route to the network is a filtering proxy inside the runner; it resolves each destination itself, connects to the address it checked, refuses private, loopback, link-local and metadata addresses for pages, redirects, subresources and WebSockets, and reaches the local fixture only through the synthetic grant. A page may load top-level pages only on the origins the flow names and may not open a second window.

A flow runs a probe like any monitor, so incidents need the same vantage agreement. Failures keep distinct reasons (expected text or element missing, no element for a click, an error status, the wall limit, a forbidden address, a second origin, a missing outcome); a runner-side problem (no browser, a missing credential) is a runner error and the flow is Unknown. An observation's `flow.digest` must equal the digest of the flow in its slot's revision. When the revision now running requires less than the revision that was current when the open incident began, a pass recovers that incident only with the changed-definition label in the state, the recovery event and the console, and the revision history shows when the required outcomes shrank. Credentials are runner-local files bound to an exact origin and appear in no table, archive, export, log, trace or audit record. The flow tables are the existing monitor, revision, slot and observation tables; no table was added.

## Alert drills

A drill exists to check, one destination at a time, that an alert reaches somebody who can act. **Run alert drill** queues the test event with drill wording (`eventType` `uptime.notification.drill`, `kind` `test`, `incidentKey` null) for that destination's current credential. Disabled destinations can be drilled, as they can be tested. The one automatic attempt and the uncertain-never-resent rule are DELIVERY-001's. The message names who started the drill and when, and carries a link to `/alert-drill` holding a random 256-bit capability. Email has it as a text line and an HTML link, Slack as a message line, and the signed JSON webhook as an extra top-level `drill` object (`startedBy`, `startedAt`, `expiresAt`, `confirmUrl`) on drill events only. Incident events, their payload shape and Lamp-compatible signing are unchanged.

D1 stores the capability's SHA-256, bound to the drill, the destination and its credential. A copy sealed under the installation key lives only until the single send is over, and the runner reads it only while it holds that delivery's claim. The stored event, the stored webhook/Slack envelope, the archive and the console reads never contain the link. Opening the link (GET or HEAD) shows a page naming only the destination kind and the drill time. Only a same-origin form POST confirms, compared in constant time and accepted once within 24 hours. Unknown, used, expired and foreign links answer the ordinary 404 page, and the path is declared in `seed.json` under its own rate-limit class. The recipient needs no sign-in.

The console shows, per destination, "Last proven" (the latest confirmed drill for the current credential), "Not proven in 30 days" or "Never proven" in words, beside the latest drill's three lines:

- **Submitted** is when Uptime started the one send. Before then it says the drill is queued, or why nothing was sent.
- **Acknowledged** is when the provider or endpoint answered and with what code, or Uncertain or Failed with the code. Acknowledgement is not inbox receipt.
- **Recipient confirmed** shows the time someone used the link, reported by the recipient. Until then it is pending, and after 24 hours it reads "not confirmed within 24 hours" with nothing sent again.

Whether anyone acted is not measured. The first-run page lists any destinations already present with the same proof words. Drill history (`alert_drills`) is installation-local: archives carry the drill's delivery history like any test send, but never the drill rows, so restored destinations start Never proven. Erasing a destination erases its drills; erasing an operator removes the operator reference from the drills they started. Unmeasured: no hosted drill to a real inbox or channel has been run (see ACCEPTANCE.md).

## Portability and retention

Versioned archives include definitions, immutable observations, state, product outbox events, notification history for source records that remain, erasure receipts, the permanent anonymised action audit, source identity and erasure journal, and an integrity digest. Legacy development sign-in records are installation-local, not archive data, and remain until their associated operator is erased or local reset; no captured-mail viewer or new sign-in capture is exposed. Restore validates bounded UTF8 bytes, schema, version, semantics and hash before transaction and requires empty product data. Existing receiving-owner, runner-registration and idle-contact records do not make product data nonempty and remain unchanged by restore. Imported monitors gain new paused current revisions; leases are interrupted and alerts quarantined. Derived attempt checkpoints, request credential values, heartbeat authority, sessions, keys, grants, contact, webhook and Slack URLs, and signing keys are installation-local and are not imported; destination definitions and retained notification histories are portable. A local CLI receiving checkout must first run `rer setup --owner-email EMAIL --non-interactive` to establish its owner and settings; setup leaves product data empty for import. No imported event or receipt automatically replays. A digest checks changed bytes, not trustworthy provenance. To restore a pre-erasure archive after an erasure, export the later source journal from Runner / Archive and provide it to restore; local CLI import accepts its absolute path in `UPTIME_LATER_JOURNAL`. By itself, an old offline archive cannot reveal later erasure when restored into a fresh installation that has never received that journal. This is a limit of Base's offline transfer model.

Observation metadata, monitor definitions, destination and delivery history, audit history, and legacy development sign-in records have no automatic age/count deletion. The maintenance sweep deletes expired operator sessions, old owner/auth rate-limit windows, expired public rate-limit rows and old login-attempt counters. Short-lived authorization rows can also expire logically without being deleted; expired discovery jobs are marked expired and retained. Drill expiry/confirmation clears only the one-time capability ciphertext/hash while retaining the drill row. Owner rotation/deletion retires endpoint credentials by scrubbing their secret material after active sends finish while keeping the credential/history row. The notification bridge removes an `integration_attempts` row only when it proves that the exact attempt never reached the send boundary; its delivery reservation and job remain. A setup migration removes stale, unclaimed dependency-approval state. Explicit owner erasure and local reset are separate destructive actions. The inspected local runtime scan compared all 76 migrated tables in a populated installation; 36 held rows before maintenance. Only aged `oa_sessions`, `oa_limits`, `login_attempts` and `public_rate_limits` rows decreased, and all other preexisting row identities survived the measured cron/lazy-expiry paths and Worker restart. This scan covers its listed execution classes, not every active automatic cleanup transition or optional provider integration. View limits (100 history slots and 200 outbox entries), monitor limits and archive limits are not deletion rules or measured storage capacity. Storage growth remains an operator responsibility. Deletion must never silently improve the configured denominator or historical health.

### Erasure and audit

Monitor erasure removes its revisions, slots, observations, derived attempt checkpoints, heartbeat claims and authority, incident events, related notification jobs and deliveries, and archive-history copies. Destination erasure removes the destination, all credential versions, dependent notification jobs, delivery attempts and outcomes, replay entries and matching archive-history copies. Operator erasure removes that operator's sessions, tokens, invitations, legacy development sign-in credentials and messages. For the original owner, the bootstrap claim keeps its singleton and claim time but replaces the operator reference with an unrelated anonymous UUID. Its receipt counts that row as anonymised, and the source journal fences its original primary key. The replacement commits in the same guarded batch as account deletion; setup remains claimed and receiving-installation authority remains local. The audit remains separate from the erased entity: its subject reference is opaque, and actor references for an erased operator become anonymised. Erasure writes a durable receipt with per-table counts and completion time in the same D1 batch as its audit record, deletion and Base portability erasure-fence statements.

The audit includes destructive actions and automated notification attempts, including test sends, plus operator authentication-mail provider attempts and legacy development capture history. The mail sender records the feature audit before attempting the provider request. Actor and subject fields are identifiers or feature names, never user-supplied names, emails, URLs or secrets. The audit travels in product archives. A later erasure journal makes restore refuse old audit actor references for an erased operator. Older offline archive bytes and external messages already sent cannot be changed by a later local erasure; a request already in progress may still reach its provider. Uptime does not use R2 or accept uploads, so there are no uploaded attachments to remove.

## Contact, interface and delivery

Authenticated runner contact records control-plane receipt time for accepted scoped claims, including idle claims, and accepted signed results. Owner reads, health checks and rejected requests do not refresh it. Fresh means within 120 seconds; future or unverifiable time is stale. Contact does not prove process liveness or target success. Restart retains it, archives omit it, and failed overview reads label contact unavailable with any prior timestamp clearly historical.

The interface exposes textual state, freshness, reasons, immutable revisions and manual provenance, with labels, keyboard controls and responsive layout. Failed reads show Unknown and last successful refresh. Action errors persist across polling; busy controls stay excluded through reconciliation. Editing must preserve configured checks; duplicated reserved typed assertion keys are rejected rather than silently overriding criteria. No fabricated uptime figure or color-only state is acceptable.

An enabled email, signed JSON webhook, or Slack incoming webhook destination applies to every monitor in that installation. The hosted Worker submits email through the configured Base mail adapter and sends webhooks and Slack without a private runner; a private runner can also claim notification work. The one-time test action queues a distinct test event and does not change monitor state. Only scheduled incident transitions create trigger and recovery notifications; maintenance suppresses these events, and manual checks do not create them. Portable archives include destination kind, name, enabled setting and available email address, but exclude webhook and Slack URLs, signing keys and other credentials. Restored destinations are disabled until the receiving owner reauthorizes and enables them.

Each event/destination pair has one automatic send attempt; a RER relay 429 with Retry-After queues an unaccepted message until that delay expires. HTTP 500, timeouts, connection loss, and unclassified responses remain uncertain without automatic resend. A successful provider or endpoint response means only that the request was acknowledged. It is not proof of inbox delivery, a person reading it, or an operational response. Authentication mail remains separate from incident email. A single runner is one network vantage; logical probe names do not establish geographic independence. CHAT-001, PHONE-001, MOBILE-001, ONCALL-001 and LAMP-001 extend the target beyond the implemented channels. Full responder rotations and public communication remain owned by the named sibling products. Browser execution, production network placement, recipient acceptance and accessibility conformance require separate validation.

Notification endpoints and webhook signing keys are authenticated encrypted
at rest under installation-local Worker-secret custody. Rotation cancels
queued work using the old credential. Once no send is in progress, retirement
scrubs endpoint, signing key and ciphertext and sets `retired_at`; the credential

Durable route-visible outcomes have been measured for exhausted HTTP, keyword
